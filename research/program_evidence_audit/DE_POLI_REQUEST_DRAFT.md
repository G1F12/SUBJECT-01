Subject: Request for de-identified cycling power and knee-extension force data (de Poli et al., EJSS 2026)

Dear authors,

I am reviewing the data requirements for a reproducible, non-interventional computational comparison of mechanical cycling work and knee-extension force before and after loading. Your article “Five Weeks of Sprint Interval Training Increase Absolute Power Output Within Severe-Intensity Domain Without Altering Muscle Activation or Fatigability” (doi:10.1002/ejsc.70089) reports both outcomes and states that supporting data may be available on reasonable request.

If sharing is possible under your consent and terms, could you provide a de-identified, linkable participant-by-session dataset for both groups, including pre/post-training timepoints: (1) assigned and recorded CWR power with sampling times and cadence, exercise start/end, cause of stopping, and total work plus its computation; (2) baseline and post-exercise knee-extension iMVC force in N, individual delay after cessation, trial selection, position and calibration; (3) PPO and RCP-PO, warm-up, body mass at each evaluation if measured, missingness/exclusion rules and participant-group mapping; (4) data dictionary, analysis code and any permitted reuse conditions? Please indicate whether temperature, post-exercise recovery or other functional tests exist, and whether any participants overlap the 2024 de Poli cohort or other public datasets. We do not require names or identifying information.

We recognize that sessions were matched by relative intensity, not necessarily absolute work, and would not infer an equal-work or whole-body intervention effect from this dataset. If only summaries can be shared, please let us know what is available.

Thank you for considering this request.
