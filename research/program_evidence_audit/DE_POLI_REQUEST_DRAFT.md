## Текст запроса (Draft Letter)

**Subject:** Request for de-identified participant-level data on cycling work and post-exercise knee-extension force (de Poli et al., Eur J Sport Sci 2026, DOI: 10.1002/ejsc.70089)

Dear Prof. Zagatto and colleagues,

I am writing to inquire about the possibility of accessing participant-level data from your recent study, *"Five Weeks of Sprint Interval Training Increase Absolute Power Output Within Severe‐Intensity Domain Without Altering Muscle Activation or Fatigability"* (*European Journal of Sport Science*, doi:10.1002/ejsc.70089).

We are conducting a non-interventional, open-science computational research program evaluating the physiological coupling between external mechanical work and post-exercise residual muscle force. Your protocol provides a valuable pairing of severe-intensity constant work rate (CWR) cycling and maximal voluntary isometric contraction (iMVC) knee extension force measured immediately pre- and post-exercise (at ~25 s post-cessation).

Under your data availability statement (*"available from the corresponding author upon reasonable request"*), we would be deeply grateful if you could share a de-identified, participant-by-session dataset covering both the SIT and control groups at pre- and post-training evaluations. The specific variables of interest are:

1. **Session & participant mapping:** Anonymized participant ID, assigned cohort (SIT vs. Control), and evaluation visit (pre-training vs. post-training).
2. **CWR exercise mechanics & work:** Target CWR power (W), recorded power series (at 5 Hz sampling, or mean power and cadence), exercise duration to task failure (seconds), and calculated total external mechanical work in Joules.
3. **Neuromuscular force measurements:** Baseline knee-extension iMVC force in Newtons, post-exercise iMVC force in Newtons, and voluntary activation (% VA) if available.
4. **Post-exercise timing:** The exact participant-specific latency / delay between cycling task cessation and the onset of the post-exercise iMVC attempt (reported group mean: 25 ± 7 s), which is essential for modeling recovery kinetics.
5. **Incremental test & anthropometric anchors:** Peak power output (PPO), respiratory compensation point power output (RCP-PO), body mass (kg), and age at each timepoint.
6. **Task termination & metadata:** Primary reason for exercise termination (voluntary exhaustion vs. cadence dropping below 60 rpm), data dictionary, and preferred citation or attribution format.

**Scope of use:**
- This research is strictly computational and non-interventional. No biological or human subject experiments are conducted by our team.
- All analyses are reproducible, open-source comparisons evaluating mechanical-to-energetic transfer functions against empirical baselines.
- No personal identifying information (PII) is requested or required.
- Any requested attribution, license terms, or data usage agreements will be strictly followed.

If sharing the full time-series is not feasible, an aggregated spreadsheet with participant-level baseline/post force, total Joules, and exact measurement delay would already provide substantial scientific utility for our investigation.

Thank you very much for your time and assistance.
